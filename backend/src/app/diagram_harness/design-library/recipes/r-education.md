# Recipe R: Education / Course

## Quick ref

- **When:** Online learning platforms, bootcamps, course landing pages, knowledge products.
- **Token budget:** ~15k (HARNESS + ergonomics + components + page-archetypes + color + anti-similarity).
- **Files to read:** `09-ergonomics.md`, `04-components.md`, `07-page-archetypes.md`, `02-color.md`
- **Personality fit:** Primary — Professional. Strong — Playful, Editorial, Warm/Human, Technical. Bad fit — Luxury, Brutalist.

## Page flow intent

Convince, then convert. The page is a sales letter, not a catalog.

Core rhythm: `video preview hero (NOT auto-play) → who it's for + outcomes → curriculum expandable accordion → instructor credibility → specific testimonials with outcomes → pricing (2-3 tiers) → FAQ → final CTA`.

Each section answers one objection. Order matters: desire → proof → logistics → price → reassurance.

## Techniques

- Course preview video as hero (not auto-play — play button)
- Curriculum as expandable accordion with lesson counts
- Instructor credibility section with real photo + credentials
- Progress indicators for enrolled students
- Testimonial cards with specific outcomes ("Got hired at X" not "Great course")
- Pricing comparison table (2-3 tiers max — Hick's Law)
- FAQ addressing objections before pricing
- Social proof: student count, completion rate, rating

## Font direction

Approachable sans for body + distinctive display for headings. Should feel expert but not intimidating.

## Color

Warm and inviting. Avoid sterile white. Cream/warm backgrounds. ONE accent for CTAs/progress. Consider the subject — a design course looks different from a coding bootcamp.

## Motion budget

LOW. Content is king. Smooth accordion expansions. Video play transitions. Nothing distracting from learning.

## Anti-patterns

Fake scarcity ("Only 3 spots left!"). Wall of testimonials. Hidden pricing. Aggressive countdown timers.
